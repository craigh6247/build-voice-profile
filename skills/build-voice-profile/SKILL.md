# Build a voice profile

Learn how a person naturally expresses themselves so future documents, texts and messages can sound like them. Use their own speech and corrections as evidence. The output is a practical writing guide, not a personality assessment, acoustic model or collection of biographical facts.

## Begin with the available context

Use existing conversation samples and a current profile if supplied. Do not restart an interview or ask the person to repeat evidence already available. Identify which situations have been sampled and which remain uncertain.

If the intended uses are unclear, ask one brief question about what they want to write and for whom. Otherwise begin with a concrete, easy question. Voice and typed answers both work; be clear that transcripts support observations about wording and reasoning, not unobserved accent, vocal pace or intonation.

## Interview naturally

- Ask one question at a time. Give a recognisable situation or invite a specific memory. Avoid prompts such as "talk naturally", "show your warmth" or "demonstrate your communication style".
- Mix everyday life and work when relevant. Sample explanation, disagreement, praise, requests, candid updates, enjoyment, stories, humour and hopes. Do not let the person's job become the whole profile.
- Use [references/interview-prompts.md](references/interview-prompts.md) when choosing questions. It contains a flexible fifteen-question route, concrete follow-ups and calibration prompts. Adapt to answers rather than administering a rigid questionnaire.
- If the person asks for a fixed number of questions, track that number and finish the agreed set unless they redirect. Keep any follow-up brief and distinguish it from the next numbered question. Do not dump the list unless asked to see it.
- Do not supply the phrases or opinions you hope to hear. A hypothetical situation is useful; a suggested answer can contaminate the sample.
- Let a short answer stand. Invite elaboration only when it adds a useful contrast or story. "Coffee" is evidence that some answers can be brief, not proof that the person's whole style is terse.
- Respond naturally without analysing or praising every answer. Repeated "that's very you" commentary interrupts the conversation and can encourage performance.
- Follow personal topics gently. Let the person decline, avoid soliciting sensitive details unnecessarily, and change direction when a question does not fit. Do not infer health, personality or relationships from a language sample.
- When someone describes frustration, distinguish their internal reaction from the message they would actually send. Ask for the sendable version when useful. Preserve both as different registers rather than treating one as inauthentic.

## Extend the interview for professional writing

When the person wants to improve customer, VC or other investor communications, use [references/professional-writing.md](references/professional-writing.md). It offers optional customer and investor modules alongside the everyday route. Select questions that fill evidence gaps, one at a time; neither route is a required quota. Reuse known recipient context and desired outcomes rather than asking for them again.

Learn how the person's own voice changes with audience, relationship and purpose. Professional writing does not require generic corporate language or another person's style. Ground drafts in supplied business facts, distinguish private intent from sendable language, and calibrate short samples for tone, specificity, the ask and certainty. Capture customer-versus-investor differences only where supported, and name untested situations explicitly.

## Build the provisional profile

Show a first version when asked or once enough varied material supports useful observations. Continue collecting samples afterwards if requested. Do not make completion of a question quota a prerequisite for producing the profile or skill.

Separate three kinds of evidence:

1. **Stated preferences:** Explicit choices about spelling, punctuation, length, tone or editing. These carry more weight than a pattern inferred from one answer.
2. **Observed patterns:** Vocabulary, connective phrases, reasoning order, sentence shapes, directness, warmth, humour and changes with the audience. Support material claims with short excerpts or specific examples. Treat one-off phrases as examples, not rules.
3. **Unknowns:** Situations not sampled, ambiguous transcriptions and interpretations that need testing. Use plain confidence descriptions rather than numerical scores or claims of certainty.

Describe what changes between private thinking, professional messages, personal texts and longer writing where evidence exists. Include how to edit the speech: what to preserve, what to remove and what would make a draft feel unlike the person.

Keep the profile concise enough to review. Useful sections are overall voice, reasoning, language, audience changes, editing guidance, anchors and gaps. Adapt the structure to the evidence. Do not force each person into the same voice or import Craig's particular spelling, phrases, humour or work context.

## Calibrate through actual writing

Draft two or three short pieces in different well-sampled situations, or one piece if that is enough for the current request. Use supplied facts; mark hypothetical material as illustrative. Do not invent feelings, promises, opinions or personal anecdotes.

Ask one specific comparison question at a time: for example, which phrase feels too formal, whether the request is too soft, or how they would say the opening themselves. Their replacement wording is stronger evidence than an inference. General approval supports the version shown; it does not prove the profile works in every context.

Revise the profile and retry the affected passage when necessary. Preserve meaning and uncertainty while removing false starts, transcription errors and accidental repetition. Do not mechanically copy fillers, catchphrases or swearing. The aim is recognisable writing, not a caricature or verbatim transcript.

## Deliver and optionally package

When asked to show the profile, display the actual profile in the current conversation using the presentation surface appropriate to the active mode. Do not merely describe a profile the person cannot see. Keep spoken commentary brief when showing written material during voice.

When asked to create a reusable writing skill, use the available skill-creator workflow. Build a distinct person-specific skill with concise instructions and a supporting file of selected voice anchors if useful. Include evidence limits and calibration guidance. Keep this interview method separate from the person's writing skill.

Retain only personal detail necessary to guide the writing. Label transcript excerpts and generated examples separately. Examples are language evidence, not authority to reuse private stories or assert current product, commercial or relationship facts. Creating a profile does not authorise sending messages, publishing it or saving unrelated personal memories.

Before declaring the work complete, check that the profile is grounded in actual samples, corrections are incorporated, unsupported claims are qualified, requested visible content is shown, and any requested skill files are created and validated. State where saved files are and distinguish the checked file structure from how well the voice has been tested in practice.
